# Plan: x402 over REST (v0.3.0)

A client pays for its call with a signed EIP-3009 authorization, through x402 v2's `exact`
scheme. Bruno is the facilitator: the authorization is settled inside the runner, in the same
transaction as the call, so it's spent only if the call succeeds and is included.

Normative text: x402 v2 (`specs/x402-specification-v2.md`), its HTTP transport
(`specs/transports-v2/http.md`), and the `exact` EVM scheme
(`specs/schemes/exact/scheme_exact_evm.md`), in github.com/coinbase/x402.

## Decisions

- **Its own endpoint:** `POST /{chain}/v1/x402`, REST only. The 402 is the quote and the
  signed authorization the acceptance: there's no separate quote or submission step.
- **Held until mined** (superseding optimistic acknowledgement, 2026-10-22): a transaction
  isn't signed until a batch maps it, then afresh each block, so there's no hash before it's
  included. A paid request is answered 200 with that hash once included, or 402 once rejected
  or expired (see Settlement signal).
- **KISS on nullifiers:** no tracking. Simulation refuses a used nonce; the same submission
  sent again has the same id. The authorization doesn't bind the call (x402 has no field
  that could), so anyone who sees it can attach it to another call: x402's exposure, not
  ours to account for. A builder dropping reverts keeps Bruno out of the way.
- **EIP-3009 only:** `transferWithAuthorization`'s `(v, r, s)` overload, which every 3009
  token has.
- **Opt-in by configuration:** a token's `eip3009: {name, version}`, its EIP-712 domain.
- **A failed transfer** reverts the runner with the token's own revert data.
- **The payee's slot never returns to 0:** the treasury converts all but one base unit of each
  3009 token. A transfer then never creates the payee's balance slot, so its cost (107,920 on
  Amsterdam, 19,900 on Prague) is left out of the bound, except while the payee holds none:
  before its first payment. The relay reads the payee's balance of each 3009 token at startup,
  and each treasury run tells it again.
- **No compromises for the ecosystem (2026-10-22):** payment and call stay atomic, in our one
  transaction. No facilitator settles for us: it would split them, and still not list us (CDP's
  supports neither Ethereum nor Sepolia). The price stays what the transaction costs, at the
  fee ceiling: no headroom cut, no runner rework to chase x402's micro-payment prices. Bazaar
  is not a channel; the declaration stays in the 402 (static, valid, free).

## Flow

1. **No `PAYMENT-SIGNATURE`:** 402. `PAYMENT-REQUIRED` (base64 JSON), the same JSON in the
   body:
   - `x402Version`: 2
   - `resource.url`: the request's URL
   - `accepts`: one entry per 3009 token: `scheme` `exact`, `network` `eip155:{chainId}`,
     `amount` the quote (the rise allowance of `postpaid_quote`), `asset`, `payTo` the payee,
     `maxTimeoutSeconds` `max_deadline_blocks` × slot, `extra` `{name, version}`.

   Reads the latest block and prices; simulates nothing; keeps nothing.
2. **With `PAYMENT-SIGNATURE`:** the payload decoded and checked:
   - `x402Version` 2, `exact`, our network;
   - the asset a 3009 token; `payTo` and `authorization.to` the payee;
   - `value` covers today's charge (the echoed `accepted.amount` isn't trusted);
   - `validAfter` before the next block.

   The deadline: the last block expected before `validBefore`, at most `max_deadline_blocks`
   ahead. Then an ordinary submission: planned, simulated once, queued.
3. **Answers:**

   | Case | Status | Headers and body |
   |---|---|---|
   | Taken (or taken already) | 200 | `PAYMENT-RESPONSE` `{success: true, transaction: "", network, payer}`; body `{id}`; `Location` the status URL |
   | Underpaid | 402 | `PAYMENT-REQUIRED` at today's price |
   | Queue full | 402 | `PAYMENT-REQUIRED` at the amount that would get in |
   | Simulation fails | 402 | `PAYMENT-RESPONSE` `{success: false, errorReason}`; body the revert data |
   | Malformed payload | 400 | the relay's error body |

4. **After that,** the existing machinery: batches, funding, bundles, status.

**Following it up.** The 200 gives the status URL three ways: `Location`, the body's
`statusUrl` (absolute) beside `id`, and `PAYMENT-RESPONSE`'s `extensions` (`{"status":
{"url": …}}`), which x402 clients pass on. It's the existing
`GET /{chain}/v1/submissions/{id}`: `pending`, then `included` with the transaction hash and
block, or `expired` or `rejected` with the reason. Kept `retention_seconds` after it
finishes. On-chain, the token's `AuthorizationUsed(from, nonce)` needs nothing from us.

CORS: `PAYMENT-SIGNATURE` allowed in; `PAYMENT-REQUIRED`, `PAYMENT-RESPONSE` exposed.

## The runner

1. Read the payee's balance.
2. `transferWithAuthorization(from, to, value, validAfter, validBefore, nonce, v, r, s)` on
   the token: its calldata a second blob after the code, `CODECOPY`'d to memory, the CALL
   given exactly the token's measured transfer gas. The token is warm from the first
   `balanceOf`. Failure reverts with the token's revert data.
3. The call, as now.
4. The balance check, as now: the payee gained at least the amount.

## The transfer's gas bound

Needed before any authorization exists, for the 402's quote.

- **Measured at startup,** in the existing batch: `eth_estimateGas` of a zero-value
  `transferWithAuthorization`, signed by a throwaway key fresh each startup, with a random
  nonce (a fixed one could be spent by anyone, breaking startup). Covers the signature check,
  the nonce's new slot and the cold reads.
- **Plus the two balance writes** a zero-value transfer leaves unchanged: the payee's from 0
  (the treasury can drain it) and the payer's changing. Per-fork figures in `GasSchedule`,
  each the EIPs' price over a warm no-op write (the balance is read first, so warm):

  | | A slot from 0 | A slot changing | Both |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Prague (EIP-2200, 2929) | 20,000 − 100 = 19,900 | 2,900 − 100 = 2,800 | 22,700 |
  | Amsterdam (EIP-8037, 8038) | 10,000 + 97,920 = 107,920 | 10,000 | 117,920 |

- **A wrong bound** reverts the runner, which is dropped: it costs nothing.

### Measured (2026-10-21)

Vendored FiatTokenV2_2 on anvil, execution gas (the estimate less intrinsic):

| | Prague | Amsterdam |
|---|---|---|
| Value 0, empty to empty | 49,236 | 138,236 |
| Value 1, holder to empty | 71,669 (+22,433) | 255,909 (+117,673) |
| Value 1, holder to holder | 54,549 (+5,313) | 157,989 (+19,753) |
| Value all, payer to 0 | 54,569 (+5,333) | 157,969 (+19,733) |

The bound covers each case, with 247–287 to spare. A real transfer's receipt matched its
estimate exactly on both forks.

Live, value 0 (`scratchpad/x402_domains.py`): Ethereum 58,103, Sepolia 148,076. That's about
9,000–10,000 over the vendored copy, from USDC's proxy, so measuring at startup against the
live token is required: no fixture-derived figure would do.

**Amsterdam makes 3009 expensive.** Every authorization's nonce is a new storage slot (97,920
state gas), so a transfer is about 148,000 at best. A payee holding none of the token adds
107,920. Together with the runner, about 490,000 gas over the call on Sepolia. Prague: about
80,000 for the transfer.

## Types

- `TransferAuthorization` (from, to, value, valid_after, valid_before, nonce, v, r, s), in a
  new `eip3009` module, with its calldata, the measuring call, and the deadline mapping.
- `Submission.authorization`, optional. The id is unchanged without one; with one, the
  authorization is appended to its encoding.
- `Erc20.transfer_gas`, for 3009 tokens.
- Wire Structs in `server/wire.py`, with x402's field names: `PaymentRequired`,
  `PaymentRequirements`, `ResourceInfo`, `PaymentPayload`, `SettlementResponse`.

## Discovery (Bazaar)

Investigated 2026-10-21. Neither catalog will list Bruno as it is: both require Base (or
Solana).

- **The `bazaar` extension** (x402 `specs/extensions/bazaar.md`): the server declares its
  endpoint (method, body schema and example, output) in the 402's `extensions.bazaar`; the
  client echoes it in its payload; the *facilitator* catalogs it when it verifies or settles.
  We're our own facilitator, so no third party sees the payload.
- **CDP Bazaar** (Coinbase's, the main channel): lists a route only after a payment to it has
  settled through CDP's facilitator, which needs a CDP API key and serves Base, Polygon,
  Arbitrum, World and Solana (and Base Sepolia, Solana Devnet). Not Ethereum, not Sepolia.
  Settling through it is also at odds with settling atomically in the runner. Routes without
  a settlement for 30 days are dropped.
- **x402scan** (Merit Systems): independent of facilitators. It registers a route by probing
  it for a 402, found through `/openapi.json` (`x-payment-info` on each paid operation),
  `/.well-known/x402`, or a URL added by hand. But it stores only `accepts` entries on Base or
  Solana (`SUPPORTED_CHAINS` in `services/db/resources/resource.ts`), and drops a resource
  with none.

**In 0.3.0 anyway,** being cheap and correct:
- `extensions.bazaar` in the 402: `POST`, `bodyType` `json`, a body example and its schema,
  an output example; `resource` with `description`, `mimeType`, `serviceName`, `tags`.
- A probe with no body, or one that doesn't parse, gets a 402, not a 400, so indexes can reach
  the challenge. It's priced for a **reference call**: one EIP-7702 authorization from an
  existing account, 150,000 gas of call, 512 bytes of calldata. That's the rule of thumb
  for "it'll probably cost this": about 340,000 gas on Ethereum (Prague), about 620,000 on
  Sepolia (Amsterdam), with the transfer. The body's `extensions.bazaar` example is that
  call, and its description says the price is a reference.
- OpenAPI's `x-payment-info` on the x402 operation (`protocols: ["x402"]`, `price` dynamic,
  in USD), and `/.well-known/x402` listing each chain's x402 URL.

### Base (investigated 2026-10-21)

Listing needs Base, and Base doesn't offer what Bruno relies on to outside senders.

- **op-rbuilder has it.** Flashbots' OP Stack builder, which builds Base's blocks, has an
  `eth_sendBundle` with revert protection behind `--builder.enable-revert-protection`. A
  bundle is exactly one transaction, valid for at most 10 blocks; one that reverts isn't
  included unless it's in `revertingTxHashes`. That's Bruno's standalone mode.
- **Base doesn't expose it.** Probed with an empty bundle (refused whatever the method's
  state, sending nothing): `mainnet-sequencer.base.org` and `sepolia-sequencer.base.org`
  answer `eth_sendBundle` with -32601 "rpc method is not allowed", as they do
  `eth_sendRawTransactionConditional`, `eth_sendPrivateTransaction` and
  `eth_sendRawTransactionSync`. `mainnet.base.org` and `sepolia.base.org` refuse it by
  "request filter settings"; publicnode doesn't have the method.
- **Base's Flashblocks launch post** (2025) said revert protection wasn't enabled in the
  first release. Nothing found since says it's open to the public.

So on Base, Bruno would need either access granted by Base (a permissioned path, against the
grain), or to run without revert protection: a submission that stops paying between its
simulation and its block would land reverted, at the relay's cost. Base's gas is cheap and
its blocks two seconds apart, so that may be a risk worth pricing, but it's a different
mechanism from the one SPEC.md defines. Not for 0.3.0.

## Measured, refreshed, degraded (added 2026-10-21)

For a relay left alone for a year: what it measures is measured again on a schedule, the fork
is measured too, and anything that fails takes out only what depends on it. A wrong schedule
lasts an hour at most. KISS: code that works 99% of the time over complex code.

- **The fork, measured.** Which schedule applies comes from an `eth_estimateGas` of an empty
  call (no `from`, no value, no data), not from activation timestamps: its answer is the
  schedule's base transaction cost, 21,000 on Prague and 15,000 on Amsterdam. The schedule
  whose prediction matches is in force; none matching is an unknown fork. `NETWORK_FORKS`'s
  timestamps go.
- **Probes checked 2026-10-21** (`scratchpad/fingerprint_probe.py`):

  | Probe | Anvil, both forks | Live (Ethereum ×2 nodes, Sepolia) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Empty call | exact | exact |
  | Calldata to an account without code | exact | 800–900 over |
  | The deployer's CREATE2 of a trivial runner | 1 under our limit | ~3,000 over |
  | 1 wei to an empty account | exact | needs a funded `from` |

  Live estimates are exact only for a plain transfer (geth's estimate stops within 1.5%,
  unverified against its source), so the empty call is the fingerprint. It can't tell a
  future fork that keeps the base cost; a schedule wrong elsewhere fails simulation (no loss).
- **Refreshed hourly.** Everything measured (the fork, each token's `balanceOf` and transfer
  gas, the payee's holdings, the deployer's presence) carries its age and is taken again once
  an hour old, when next used: an idle relay reads nothing. The Worker's hourly cron refreshes
  each chain's object it wakes; each wake re-measures, as now.
- **Degrade, never die.**
  - A chain that can't open is retried with backoff; its paths answer 503 with why. The Python
    server starts whatever fails.
  - A token whose measurement fails is suspended (refused, with why), the rest served; retried
    at the next refresh.
  - An unknown fork, or the deployer missing (its code not the runtime), stops new submissions
    and the treasury's runs, with why.
  - The root shows each chain's and token's state, and why.
- **The deployer, checked.** Found 2026-10-21: it isn't deployed on Ethereum. A transaction to
  an address without code succeeds, so a runner never ran, the payment was never checked, and
  the simulation passed: production's Ethereum float was open to anyone. The deployer's code
  is read with the measurements; a simulation whose output isn't the runner's address (32
  bytes) fails.

## Settlement signal and conformance (added 2026-10-21)

The acknowledgement (200, `transaction` empty) isn't what x402's HTTP transport means by 200
("payment verified and settled"): an off-the-shelf client takes it as settled and never learns
the outcome; a wallet has to poll a Bruno URL. Decided:

- **Hold by default.** A paid x402 request is answered when its submission finishes: 200 with
  the real `transaction` once included (spec-exact), 402 `success: false` once rejected or
  expired (the authorization unused). Waiting costs no RPC: the held request is a parked
  in-memory promise, resolved by the settlement the relay runs each block anyway (about 5
  batched reads a block while anything is pending, however many wait). It's fewer billed
  requests than polling. A dropped connection (a deploy restarting the object) falls back to
  the status URL, or the same payment sent again, answered as it stands.
- **Always held (2026-10-22): no 202, no opt-out.** x402's HTTP transport has 200 "verified and
  settled", 402, 400 and 500, nothing asynchronous. Progress is the status URL (`Location`),
  read alongside the held request.
- **x402 deadlines capped at about 10 blocks** (`maxTimeoutSeconds` says so): the hold is
  bounded; past it, expired, 402, the client signs again. Clients set their HTTP timeout to
  at least `maxTimeoutSeconds` (httpx's default is 5 s): README.md says so for wallet developers.
- **The stage lifecycle** on the status endpoint, for everyone, `status` kept as it is:

  | Stage | Meaning |
  |---|---|
  | `waiting` | taken, not yet sent (a batch, its account's funding) |
  | `sending` | offered to builders for block N, attempt k |
  | `confirming` | in block B, its transaction hash, `c` confirmations |
  | `done` | its block finalized |
  | `failed` | rejected (why) or expired |

  `Retry-After` on it: when the stage could next change. `confirming`'s count and `done` read
  the head and the finalized block at most once a slot, only when asked.
- **One paid call at a time per payer:** a payment for the same payer, token, call, gas and
  authorizations while one is pending waits on that one, its own authorization never used, and
  is answered with its outcome. A client that times out and signs again pays once.
- **No read caches or waiter caps.** They could bite legitimate clients when the relay is
  busiest, for floods the edge should stop. Flood protection is a Cloudflare rate-limiting rule
  on `/*/v1/*` (DEPLOYMENT.md: required for a public deployment). Revisited with evidence.

**Conformance, before any x402 deploy:**
1. **Normative:** a checklist of x402 v2's MUSTs and SHOULDs (core, HTTP transport, `exact` on
   EVM, `bazaar`), each a test or a documented deviation: amounts (any value covering today's
   charge), verification by simulation, settlement inside the call's transaction.
2. **Interoperability:** x402's reference Python client (the `x402` package, a test-only
   dependency pinned in uv.lock; `register_exact_evm_client(..., networks="eip155:*")`) pays a
   call through each relay on anvil, unmodified: a POST with a JSON body, held to 200 with its
   transaction. To check first: the release implementing v2, and that its httpx client resends
   a POST with its body.
3. **Discovery:** the bazaar declaration valid by its schema; x402scan's rules (a bodyless probe
   answered 402, non-empty `accepts`, atomic amounts, an input schema, `https` behind a proxy).

**Free-plan exposure, for the record:** funds aren't reachable by floods (every gas-spending
path needs a paying submission that simulates). A flood can burn the 100,000 requests a day
(an outage until midnight UTC, no bill) or the public RPCs' rate limits (degraded until it
stops). Worker CPU is 10 ms an invocation on the free plan: to be measured on the deployed
Worker per alarm before relying on it under load.
